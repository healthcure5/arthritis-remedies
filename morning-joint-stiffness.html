How to ease morning joint stiffness: 9 gentle things to try

Stiff knees, swollen-feeling fingers, a back that won't straighten. Here's why the first hour is the hardest, and the small changes that help.

Key points

  • Morning stiffness happens because joints sit still for hours while you sleep. Movement and warmth loosen them again.
  • Start in bed: ankle circles, hand squeezes and heel slides before you stand.
  • A warm shower, a warm mug and a slower start make the first half hour easier.
  • Stiffness lasting more than 30 minutes most mornings is worth telling your doctor about.

You wake up, and before your feet touch the floor you already know what kind of morning it's going to be. Knees that won't bend all the way. Fingers that feel two sizes too big. A back that needs a minute before it agrees to stand up straight.

If that's your first hour, you're far from alone. In one study, morning pain and stiffness was the arthritis symptom with the biggest effect on people's quality of life.1 The good news is that mornings respond well to small changes, and most of them start the night before.

Why your joints are stiffest when you wake up

While you sleep, your joints barely move for hours. The fluid that cushions them thickens, and the muscles around them cool and tighten. Movement and warmth loosen everything again, which is why stiffness fades as the morning goes on.

How long it lasts is a clue worth paying attention to. Harvard Health says morning stiffness from osteoarthritis, the wear-and-tear kind, usually lasts about 10 to 15 minutes. Stiffness from rheumatoid arthritis can last more than an hour.2

Stiff for more than half an hour most mornings? That's worth a conversation with your doctor.

Before your feet touch the floor

  1. Move while you're still lying downCircle your ankles, open and close your hands, and slide each heel toward you and back a few times. Members of one rheumatoid arthritis community say slow stretches in bed are what help them most.3
  2. Sit on the edge of the bed for a minuteLet your body catch up before you stand. Roll your shoulders and turn your head gently from side to side.
  3. Keep a glass of water by the bedDrinking it before you stand is an easy first habit, and it gives your body a moment to wake up.

The first half hour

  1. Take a warm shower or bathWarm water relaxes tight muscles and loosens joints. The Arthritis Foundation suggests a hot shower or a heating pad for a softer start.4 A shower stool is perfectly fine.
  2. Warm your hands before you grip anythingRun them under warm water, or hold a warm mug in both hands, before you open jars or button a shirt.
  3. Give yourself extra timeRushing makes stiff joints feel worse. Get up 20 minutes earlier, or lay out clothes the night before.
  4. Set up the kitchen the night beforeKeep the mug, the kettle and the coffee where you don't have to reach or bend, and loosen jar lids before bed.

Start the night before

  1. Move a little before bedA short, easy walk or a few gentle stretches in the evening can mean less stiffness in the morning.
  2. Keep your joints warm overnightA warmer bedroom, soft socks, or a blanket over sore knees helps many people wake up less stiff. A warm castor oil pack in the evening is another ritual people enjoy.

Stiff knees, hands, hips and back

Stiff knees in the morning, stiff hands and fingers, tight hips or an achy lower back all respond to the same three things: gentle movement before you stand, warmth, and a slower start. Whether you call it achy joints when you wake up, joint pain in the morning or simply feeling rusty, the steps above are a safe place to begin. They suit older adults and seniors, and they're easy to set up for an elderly parent too.

Track it for two weeks

Each morning, write down how many minutes it takes before you feel loose. You'll see what's helping, and you'll have real numbers to show your doctor.

When to call your doctor

See your doctor if stiffness lasts longer than 30 minutes most mornings, if a joint is swollen, hot or red, if you have a fever, or if pain keeps you awake. Keep taking anything you've been prescribed, and only change a medicine after speaking with your doctor.

Questions people ask

Why are my joints so stiff when I wake up?

Lying still for hours lets the fluid that cushions your joints thicken and the muscles around them cool and tighten. Gentle movement warms everything up again, so stiffness usually eases as the morning goes on.

How long should morning stiffness last?

With osteoarthritis it usually lasts about 10 to 15 minutes, according to Harvard Health. With rheumatoid arthritis it can last more than an hour. If yours lasts longer than 30 minutes most mornings, tell your doctor.

What is the fastest way to loosen stiff joints in the morning?

Move gently while you're still in bed, then take a warm shower or use a heating pad. Circling your ankles and opening and closing your hands before you stand helps many people.

What helps stiff knees and hips in the morning?

Slide each heel toward you and back a few times before getting up, sit on the edge of the bed for a minute, and use warmth. A short, easy walk in the evening can also mean less stiffness the next morning.

What helps stiff hands and fingers in the morning?

Run your hands under warm water or hold a warm mug with both hands, then slowly open and close your fingers. Loosening jar lids the night before saves your hands in the morning.

How can I help an elderly parent with morning joint stiffness?

Help them set up an easier morning: water by the bed, a shower stool, clothes laid out the night before, and kitchen items within easy reach. Encourage gentle movement before standing, and a doctor visit if stiffness lasts longer than 30 minutes.
