Do arthritis gloves help?

Good morning {$name|default('there')},

You've probably seen arthritis gloves: snug, fingerless gloves sold to ease hand pain and stiffness. They're comfortable, and some people swear by them. A team at the University of Salford in England tested them properly.

They recruited 206 people with rheumatoid arthritis or a similar inflammatory arthritis and ongoing hand pain. Half got snug arthritis compression gloves. The other half got loose-fitting gloves that looked similar but didn't squeeze. Nobody was told which kind was the 'real' one.

After 12 weeks, both groups said their hand pain was a little better, by about one point on a ten-point scale. There was no real difference between the snug gloves and the loose ones, and no difference in stiffness or how well people could use their hands. The study was published in BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders in 2021, and the researchers advised the UK's health service to stop handing the gloves out routinely.

So if you own a pair and they feel nice and warm, keep wearing them. Warmth is pleasant. Just don't spend much on new ones expecting the squeeze itself to fix anything.

Source: Hammond et al., BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders (2021)

THIS WEEK'S ROUTINE

This week: your warm water and five moves, plus ten thumb presses. Roll up a sock into a ball and press your thumb into it, hold for three seconds, release.

  1. Warm your hands in a bowl of warm water for two minutes (warm, never hot; test it with your wrist).
  2. Finger spread: open your hand as wide as it goes, hold for three seconds, then relax. Five times.
  3. Loose fist: curl your fingers into a soft fist, thumb on the outside, then open. Five times.
  4. Thumb to fingertips: touch your thumb to the tip of each finger, one by one, like counting. Twice through.
  5. Tabletop: keep your fingers straight and bend only at the big knuckles, so your hand makes a table shape. Five times.
  6. Finger walk: rest your hand flat on the table and walk your fingers slowly toward your thumb, one at a time. Twice.

Move slowly. A light stretch is fine. Stop if anything feels sharp, and skip any joint that is hot, red or very swollen.

THIS WEEK'S RECIPE

Ginger carrot soup

Makes 4 bowls · 30 minutes

Bright, warming and smooth, made with frozen sliced carrots and a jar of ready-minced ginger.

You'll need

  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 bag frozen chopped onion (about 10 oz)
  • 1 bag frozen sliced carrots (about 16 oz)
  • 1 tablespoon jarred minced ginger
  • 4 cups low-sodium vegetable broth
  • 1/2 cup canned coconut milk
  • Salt and pepper

How to make it

  1. Warm the oil in a pot and cook the onion for 5 minutes.
  2. Add the carrots, ginger and broth. Simmer for 20 minutes, until the carrots are very soft.
  3. Let it cool a little, then blend until smooth. A stick blender saves lifting a hot pot.
  4. Stir in the coconut milk and season to taste.
