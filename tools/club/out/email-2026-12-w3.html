What really helped sore thumbs

Good morning {$name|default('there')},

The joint at the base of the thumb is one of the most common places for hand arthritis. It aches when you turn a key, open a jar or pinch something small. Thumb splints are often handed out for it, so a UK team decided to test whether they add anything.

They enrolled 349 adults with thumb-base arthritis at 17 NHS hospitals and split them into three groups. Everyone got a self-management program from a therapist: how to protect the joint, exercises, and easier ways to do daily jobs. One group also got a real thumb splint, one got a fake splint, and one got no splint at all.

All three groups improved. And the splint made no extra difference to hand pain after eight weeks. The study was published in the journal Rheumatology in 2021.

Here's the part worth holding onto: the people got better from the self-management program itself, the exercises and the smarter ways of doing things. That's exactly what this month's routine and weekend challenges are. If a splint helps you, keep using it. But the daily habits are doing the heavy lifting.

Source: Adams et al., Rheumatology (2021)

IS IT TRUE?

Does a thumb splint help thumb arthritis?

In a 2021 trial of 349 people, adding a splint to a self-management program made no extra difference to hand pain. The program itself is what helped.

Verdict: Fine if you like it, but the daily habits matter more.

THIS WEEK'S ROUTINE

This week: warm water, five moves and thumb presses, plus ten slow wrist turns each way, like stirring a pot.

  1. Warm your hands in a bowl of warm water for two minutes (warm, never hot; test it with your wrist).
  2. Finger spread: open your hand as wide as it goes, hold for three seconds, then relax. Five times.
  3. Loose fist: curl your fingers into a soft fist, thumb on the outside, then open. Five times.
  4. Thumb to fingertips: touch your thumb to the tip of each finger, one by one, like counting. Twice through.
  5. Tabletop: keep your fingers straight and bend only at the big knuckles, so your hand makes a table shape. Five times.
  6. Finger walk: rest your hand flat on the table and walk your fingers slowly toward your thumb, one at a time. Twice.

Move slowly. A light stretch is fine. Stop if anything feels sharp, and skip any joint that is hot, red or very swollen.

THIS WEEK'S RECIPE

Peanut noodle bowl

Serves 2 · 15 minutes

Dinner in one pot with a bag of pre-shredded cabbage, so there's nothing to cut.

You'll need

  • 4 oz whole-wheat spaghetti or rice noodles
  • 1 bag pre-shredded coleslaw cabbage (about 10 oz)
  • 3 tablespoons smooth peanut butter
  • 2 tablespoons low-sodium soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon lime juice (bottled is fine)
  • 1 teaspoon jarred minced ginger
  • 2 to 3 tablespoons warm water

How to make it

  1. Cook the noodles. Add the cabbage to the pot for the last minute, then drain everything together in a colander.
  2. In a bowl, stir the peanut butter, soy sauce, lime juice, ginger and warm water into a smooth sauce.
  3. Toss the noodles and cabbage in the sauce. Eat warm or cold.
