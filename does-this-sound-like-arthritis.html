Does this sound like arthritis? Eight everyday stories

Short, familiar stories of joint trouble, and the kind of problem each can point to. Use them to find words for what you feel before you see a doctor.

The short answer

  • Many people describe their joint trouble as a story: "I wake up stiff," "my toe woke me at 3 a.m."
  • Each pattern can point toward certain conditions, but many overlap.
  • Only a doctor can say what is happening. These stories help you describe it.
  • A hot, swollen joint with a fever is never a "wait and see" problem.

Most people don't say "I have a degenerative joint condition." They say, "My hands are stiff when I get up," or "I can't get my wedding ring off anymore." Here are eight stories like that, with what each can point to. They are patterns, not verdicts.

1. "I wake up stiff, but it eases up after I get moving."

Stiffness that clears within about 30 minutes is the pattern NIAMS describes for osteoarthritis, which often affects hands, knees, hips and the back.1 See stiff hands in the morning.

2. "Both my hands are stiff for over an hour, and I'm worn out."

Longer morning stiffness in matching joints on both sides, with tiredness, is a pattern linked with rheumatoid arthritis.2 It is a reason to see a doctor soon rather than wait. Read rheumatoid arthritis.

3. "My big toe woke me at 3 a.m., red, hot and too sore for the sheet to touch."

Sudden night-time pain, swelling, redness and warmth, often in the big toe, is the classic picture of gout.3 Read gout. If it comes with a fever, get care right away.

4. "One whole finger swelled up like a sausage. I also have scaly skin patches and pitted nails."

A whole swollen finger or toe, plus psoriasis and nail changes, is a pattern NIAMS lists for psoriatic arthritis.4 Read psoriatic arthritis.

5. "My lower back has hurt for months. It's worst after sitting and better when I move."

Slowly worsening back pain that is worse after rest and improves with exercise, especially starting young, is a clue the Mayo Clinic lists for ankylosing spondylitis.5 Read ankylosing spondylitis.

6. "My thumb-side fingers go numb at night and I shake my hand to wake it up."

Numbness and tingling in the thumb, index and middle fingers, often worse at night, is a classic sign of carpal tunnel syndrome, which affects a nerve in the wrist rather than the joints.6 It is common to mix up with arthritis, which is why a doctor's view helps.

7. "My groin and thigh ache, and I've started to limp."

Pain in the groin or thigh with a limp can come from the hip, and the Mayo Clinic lists hip pain among the places osteoarthritis can cause trouble.7 A doctor can check the hip and decide on tests. See stiff hips and knees when walking.

8. "My knees ache on the stairs and after a long day on my feet."

Pain that builds with use and eases with rest is a pattern NIAMS describes for osteoarthritis.1 See why stairs are so hard.

None of these fit?

That's fine. Real life is messier than any list. Try what joint pain can mean for the details that matter most, and the arthritis A to Z for any new words.

Questions people ask

How do I know if my joint pain is arthritis?

You can't tell from symptoms alone. Patterns such as morning stiffness, swelling and which joints are affected are clues, and a doctor uses history, an exam and sometimes tests.

Does morning stiffness mean arthritis?

It can. Stiffness under 30 minutes fits osteoarthritis, and longer stiffness fits inflammatory types like rheumatoid arthritis. A doctor can tell which.

Could numb fingers at night be arthritis?

Possibly not. Numbness in the thumb-side fingers is a classic sign of carpal tunnel syndrome, a wrist nerve problem. A doctor can sort it out.

What does gout feel like?

Sudden, intense pain with swelling, redness and warmth, often in the big toe, often starting at night.

Which story sounds most like mine?

Only a doctor can say. Use the stories to describe your symptoms, and write down when and where it hurts.
