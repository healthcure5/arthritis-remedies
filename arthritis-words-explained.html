Arthritis A to Z: the hard words, in plain English

Long words in a doctor's office can stop anyone cold. These are the common ones, each in a line or two, so you can keep up and ask better questions.

The short answer

  • You don't need to memorize anything. Use it to look things up.
  • Definitions are short and based on NIAMS, the CDC and the Mayo Clinic.
  • If a word is about your own diagnosis, ask your doctor to explain it too. That is always allowed.

A long word in a doctor's office can make you stop listening, right when you most need to follow along. Keep this short list beside you.

The conditions

  • Arthritis: a word for joint pain and joint disease. The CDC says there are more than 100 types.1
  • Osteoarthritis (OA): the most common type. The joint tissues gradually break down over time.2 See osteoarthritis.
  • Rheumatoid arthritis (RA): an autoimmune disease in which the immune system attacks the lining of the joints.3 See rheumatoid arthritis.
  • Gout: an inflammatory arthritis caused by urate crystals in and around a joint.4 See gout.
  • Psoriatic arthritis: an inflammatory arthritis linked to psoriasis.5 See psoriatic arthritis.
  • Ankylosing spondylitis (axial spondyloarthritis): an inflammatory disease that mainly affects the spine.6 See ankylosing spondylitis.

The body words

  • Cartilage: the smooth tissue that cushions the ends of bones in a joint.
  • Inflammation: the body's response that can cause swelling, warmth, redness and pain.
  • Autoimmune: when the immune system mistakenly attacks the body's own tissue.3
  • Range of motion: how far a joint can move.
  • Crepitus: a crackling or grinding feel or sound when a joint moves.
  • Entheses: the places where tendons and ligaments attach to bone.5

The signs

  • Flare: a time when symptoms get worse, followed by calmer periods.
  • Heberden's and Bouchard's nodes: firm bumps near the finger joints in hand osteoarthritis.7
  • Urate: the substance that can form crystals in gout.4
  • Tophi: firm lumps of urate crystals under the skin, from long-standing gout.8
  • Uveitis: eye inflammation. It needs prompt care.5
  • HLA-B27: a gene linked with ankylosing spondylitis. Many people who have it never develop the disease.6

The people and the care

  • Rheumatologist: a doctor who specializes in arthritis and related conditions.
  • Physical therapist: helps you move and strengthen safely.
  • Occupational therapist: helps with daily tasks, grip aids and joint-friendly ways of doing things.
  • Orthotics: shoe inserts or braces that support a joint.
  • Joint protection: ways of using joints that lower strain, such as using bigger joints for lifting and avoiding repeated motions.
  • NSAIDs: non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, such as ibuprofen and naproxen. Ask your doctor or pharmacist what is right for you.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between arthritis and osteoarthritis?

Arthritis is the general word for joint disease. Osteoarthritis is one type, the most common, in which joint tissues gradually break down.

What is a rheumatologist?

A doctor who specializes in arthritis and related conditions, including autoimmune types like rheumatoid arthritis.

What is a flare?

A period when symptoms worsen, usually followed by calmer periods.

What is an autoimmune disease?

A condition where the immune system mistakenly attacks the body's own tissue, as in rheumatoid arthritis.

What are tophi?

Firm lumps of urate crystals under the skin that can form with long-standing gout.
