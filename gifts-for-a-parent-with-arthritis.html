Gifts for a parent with arthritis: useful, kind and not another bottle

What to give Mom or Dad when their hands ache and their knees complain. Tools that take the strain off, warmth, your time, and a few things to skip.

The Hinge Care team

The short answer

  • The best gifts take strain off hands and joints, or give your time. Large-handled tools, a jar opener, an electric can opener and lever door handles come straight from Mayo Clinic's joint protection list.
  • Heat helps many people, so a heating pad or warm shower products are practical.
  • Specific help beats open offers: groceries, rides, dinner, laundry or the lawn.
  • Skip gifts that remind them of what they can't do, and think twice about supplements.
  • Ask them, or their doctor or an occupational therapist, before buying anything medical.

You want to give something that says "I see how hard mornings are," without saying it out loud. It's a delicate gift to get right. Here are ideas that are practical, kind, and unlikely to end up in the back of a cupboard.

Tools that take the strain

Mayo Clinic's joint protection list for hand arthritis reads like a gift guide.1 Pick one or two, not a whole set:

  • A jar opener with a large cushioned grip, or one that mounts under a cabinet
  • An electric can opener
  • Vegetable peelers, spatulas and serving spoons with larger, cushioned handles
  • Knives with offset handles, and a cutting board with pegs that hold food steady
  • Foam tubing for handles, or an electric toothbrush
  • A lever for the door handle, and a large turning tool for keys
  • Large-barrel pens with rubber grips
  • Slip-on shoes and a long-handled shoehorn

The Arthritis Foundation suggests an occupational therapist as the person to ask about assistive devices and easier ways to do daily tasks.2 If your parent is open to it, offering to go along to that appointment is a gift of its own.

Warmth

Heat can temporarily ease joint pain. Mayo Clinic lists heating pads and warm baths or showers, and limits heating pad use to 20 minutes at a time.3 A heating pad with a safe auto shut-off, or a soft throw for the evening chair, is a thoughtful, low-risk choice. See heat or ice before you shop.

Help that has a date on it

The Arthritis Foundation recommends specific offers over general ones. Pick up prescriptions or groceries, share the driving, do the laundry, mow the lawn, or bring dinner, after asking about dietary needs.2 "I'm coming Saturday to bring lunch and do your pots" is easier to say yes to than "let me know if you need anything." You could write it on a card.

Something to read, something to do

If your parent likes to read, a large-print book is an easy gift to give. Hinge Care publishes our own: The Joint Reset, a large-print book on which home remedies are worth the money, and the Hinge Care Program, a program built on one short step a day. We make these, so weigh that as you like. For a gift in someone else's name, see our gift page.

What to skip

  • Anything that reminds them of what they can't do, like a gift that says "senior" in huge letters.
  • Supplements, unless they've asked. The best-known ones, glucosamine and chondroitin, haven't shown clear benefit in large studies, and some interact with medicines. See what the research says.
  • Advice dressed as a gift. A book of exercises can feel like a nudge. Offer it and let them choose.

Questions people ask

What is a good gift for someone with arthritis?

Tools that reduce strain on joints, like cushioned-handle kitchen tools, a jar opener and an electric can opener. Warmth, like a heating pad, and your time are also well received.

What gifts help with arthritis in the hands?

Mayo Clinic lists larger cushioned handles on utensils, a jar opener, an electric can opener, lever door handles, large-barrel pens and spring-loaded scissors.

What should I not give someone with arthritis?

Skip gifts that stress their limits and think twice about supplements. Ask a doctor or pharmacist first, because some interact with medicines.

What can I do for a parent with arthritis besides buy things?

Offer specific help: groceries, rides, dinner, laundry or yard work. The Arthritis Foundation says specific offers are easier to accept than open ones.

Are heating pads safe for older adults?

Used for no more than 20 minutes at a time and with the skin checked, they are commonly recommended. Ask a doctor if your parent has diabetes or numb skin.
