Hearing a long word in a doctor's office is stressful. Here is a short list, in plain words, to keep beside you.
@body
## The conditions {#conditions}

- **Arthritis:** a word for joint pain and joint disease. The CDC says there are more than 100 types.[^1]
- **Osteoarthritis (OA):** the most common type. The joint tissues gradually break down over time.[^2] See [osteoarthritis](osteoarthritis.html).
- **Rheumatoid arthritis (RA):** an autoimmune disease in which the immune system attacks the lining of the joints.[^3] See [rheumatoid arthritis](rheumatoid-arthritis.html).
- **Gout:** an inflammatory arthritis caused by urate crystals in and around a joint.[^4] See [gout](gout.html).
- **Psoriatic arthritis:** an inflammatory arthritis linked to psoriasis.[^5] See [psoriatic arthritis](psoriatic-arthritis.html).
- **Ankylosing spondylitis (axial spondyloarthritis):** an inflammatory disease that mainly affects the spine.[^6] See [ankylosing spondylitis](ankylosing-spondylitis.html).

## The body words {#body}

- **Cartilage:** the smooth tissue that cushions the ends of bones in a joint.
- **Inflammation:** the body's response that can cause swelling, warmth, redness and pain.
- **Autoimmune:** when the immune system mistakenly attacks the body's own tissue.[^3]
- **Range of motion:** how far a joint can move.
- **Crepitus:** a crackling or grinding feel or sound when a joint moves.
- **Entheses:** the places where tendons and ligaments attach to bone.[^5]

## The signs {#signs}

- **Flare:** a time when symptoms get worse, followed by calmer periods.
- **Heberden's and Bouchard's nodes:** firm bumps near the finger joints in hand osteoarthritis.[^7]
- **Urate:** the substance that can form crystals in gout.[^4]
- **Tophi:** firm lumps of urate crystals under the skin, from long-standing gout.[^8]
- **Uveitis:** eye inflammation. It needs prompt care.[^5]
- **HLA-B27:** a gene linked with ankylosing spondylitis. Many people who have it never develop the disease.[^6]

## The people and the care {#care}

- **Rheumatologist:** a doctor who specializes in arthritis and related conditions.
- **Physical therapist:** helps you move and strengthen safely.
- **Occupational therapist:** helps with daily tasks, grip aids and joint-friendly ways of doing things.
- **Orthotics:** shoe inserts or braces that support a joint.
- **Joint protection:** ways of using joints that lower strain, such as using bigger joints for lifting and avoiding repeated motions.
- **NSAIDs:** non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, such as ibuprofen and naproxen. Ask your doctor or pharmacist what is right for you.

@faq
Q: What is the difference between arthritis and osteoarthritis?
A: Arthritis is the general word for joint disease. Osteoarthritis is one type, the most common, in which joint tissues gradually break down.
Q: What is a rheumatologist?
A: A doctor who specializes in arthritis and related conditions, including autoimmune types like rheumatoid arthritis.
Q: What is a flare?
A: A period when symptoms worsen, usually followed by calmer periods.
Q: What is an autoimmune disease?
A: A condition where the immune system mistakenly attacks the body's own tissue, as in rheumatoid arthritis.
Q: What are tophi?
A: Firm lumps of urate crystals under the skin that can form with long-standing gout.
