## What is ankylosing spondylitis? {#what}

The Mayo Clinic says ankylosing spondylitis is an inflammatory disease that mainly affects the spine. Over time, inflammation can cause vertebrae to fuse, reducing flexibility and sometimes leading to a hunched posture. There are two forms: AS, which shows on X-ray, and nonradiographic axial spondyloarthritis, diagnosed through symptoms, blood tests and imaging such as MRI.[^1]

## What are the signs? {#signs}

- Onset usually in early adulthood
- Back pain and stiffness, mainly in the lower back and hips, often worse in the morning or after inactivity
- Neck pain and fatigue
- Eye pain or vision changes, skin rashes or stomach pain in some people
- Symptoms that flare and then ease[^1]

## When to see a doctor {#see}

The Mayo Clinic lists slowly worsening low back or buttock pain, and pain that is worse in the morning, wakes you at night, improves with exercise and worsens with rest.[^1]

Get an eye specialist right away for a red, painful eye, light sensitivity or blurry vision, because it can mean eye inflammation.[^1]

## Causes and outlook {#outlook}

The exact cause is unknown, though genes play a role. A gene called HLA-B27 greatly raises risk, but many people with the gene never develop AS, and some people with AS don't have it.[^1] Possible complications include eye inflammation, spinal compression fractures, heart problems and stiff rib joints that make deep breathing harder.[^1]

There's no cure. Treatment aims to reduce symptoms, manage pain and possibly slow progression.[^1] Staying active is part of care, and a rheumatologist can guide the plan. See our page on [exercise and arthritis](will-exercise-make-arthritis-worse.html).
