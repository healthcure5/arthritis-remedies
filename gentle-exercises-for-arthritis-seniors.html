Gentle exercises for arthritis: 6 easy chair moves for stiff joints

When moving hurts, it's tempting to stop. Here's how to start again gently, with six chair exercises you can do in your living room.

Key points

  • The CDC says being active can cut arthritis pain and improve function by about 40%.
  • Start low and go slow: even 3 to 5 minutes twice a day is a real start.
  • Six simple chair exercises work the hips, knees, ankles, shoulders and hands.
  • Walking, biking, swimming and water exercise are joint-friendly choices.

Moving hurts, so you move less. Moving less makes the joints stiffer, so moving hurts more. If that loop sounds familiar, you're not stuck in it for good.

The CDC says adults with arthritis can cut pain and improve how well they move by about 40% just by being physically active.1 You don't need a gym, special clothes or a single pound of weights. You need a sturdy chair, a little floor space, and a pace that feels kind.

Why gentle movement helps stiff joints

Long gone are the days when people with arthritis were told to rest their joints. Movement keeps joints nourished, keeps the muscles around them strong enough to share the load, and helps with balance.

40%Adults with arthritis can reduce pain and improve function by about 40% by being physically active, according to the CDC.1

The CDC suggests working up to 150 minutes a week of moderate activity, like brisk walking, plus two days of muscle-strengthening and some balance work.2 If that sounds like a lot, it also says this: some activity is better than none.2

How to start when everything aches

The CDC's advice is to start low and go slow. Someone who hasn't been active can begin with just 3 to 5 minutes, twice a day, and add a little more once the body adjusts.3

The two-hour rule many people use

Mild soreness is normal when you start something new. If a joint hurts more two hours after moving than it did before, do a little less next time.

6 gentle chair exercises for stiff joints

Sit near the front of a sturdy chair with no wheels, feet flat on the floor. Move slowly, breathe normally, and stop anything that causes sharp pain.

  1. Seated marchingLift one knee a few inches, lower it, then the other. Keep going for 30 seconds. It warms up the hips and knees.
  2. Knee straighteningSlowly straighten one leg until it's level with the chair, hold for a breath, and lower. Five times each side builds the muscles that protect the knee.
  3. Ankle circlesLift one foot slightly and draw slow circles, five each way. Then switch feet.
  4. Shoulder rollsRoll both shoulders up, back and down, five times. Then five times forward.
  5. Hand open and closeMake a soft fist, then spread your fingers wide. Ten times, slowly. Wonderful for stiff fingers.
  6. Sit to standScoot forward, lean slightly over your feet, and stand up using your legs. Sit back down slowly. Start with three. This is one of the most useful strength moves for everyday life.

Walking, water and other joint-friendly activities

Joint-friendly activities are low-impact, meaning they don't pound or twist the joints. The CDC lists walking, biking and swimming as examples.2 Water exercise is a favorite for many people with sore knees and hips, because the water supports your weight.

Short walks count. Two 10-minute walks a day add up quickly, and a walk after dinner can mean less stiffness the next morning. For the first hour of the day, see our guide to easing morning joint stiffness.

Staying safe

Talk to your doctor before starting a new exercise routine, especially after surgery, a fall or a recent flare. Stop if a joint becomes hot, red or very swollen, or if you feel chest pain, dizziness or shortness of breath.

Questions people ask

What is the best exercise for arthritis in seniors?

Low-impact activities like walking, swimming, water exercise and cycling are joint-friendly choices the CDC recommends, along with simple strength and balance exercises. The best one is the one you'll keep doing.

Are chair exercises good for arthritis?

Yes. Chair exercises let older adults build strength and loosen joints without standing for long or getting down on the floor. Seated marching, knee straightening and sit-to-stand are good places to start.

How much exercise should someone with arthritis do?

The CDC suggests working up to 150 minutes a week of moderate activity plus two days of strength and balance work. If that's too much, start with a few minutes at a time. Some activity is better than none.

Should I exercise if my joints hurt?

Mild soreness is common when you start. Choose gentle, low-impact moves and go slowly. If a joint is hot, red or very swollen, rest and talk to your doctor.

What exercises should I avoid with knee arthritis?

High-impact moves like jumping and running on hard surfaces, and deep squats or kneeling, can be hard on sore knees. Ask your doctor or a physical therapist which moves suit you.

Can exercise help arthritis in the hands?

Gentle hand exercises, like slowly opening and closing a soft fist and spreading the fingers, can help keep fingers moving. Warm the hands in warm water first.
