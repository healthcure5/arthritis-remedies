The castor oil pack: an old remedy for sore joints, and what the research really says

An old, simple remedy for aching knees and hands. Here's how to do it safely, and what the science actually shows.

The 30-second version

  • A castor oil pack is a warm, oil-soaked cloth laid over a sore joint for 30 to 60 minutes.
  • Test your skin first, use gentle warmth, and never sleep on a heating pad.
  • Research on packs is thin. The best-known study used castor oil capsules, not packs.
  • Think of it as a soothing ritual, not a treatment. Skip it on broken skin, hot swollen joints, or during pregnancy.

Long before heating pads, people eased sore joints with a warm, oil-soaked cloth. A castor oil pack is that same remedy: castor oil on soft flannel, laid over an aching knee or hand, and kept warm while you rest.

Natural health teachers such as Barbara O'Neill have taught it for years, and for many people it becomes the calmest half hour of their evening. Here's exactly how to make one, who should skip it, and an honest look at what the research does and doesn't show.

What you need

  • Castor oil. Cold-pressed is the usual choice.
  • Soft cotton or wool flannel, big enough to cover the joint when folded in two or three layers
  • Plastic wrap or an old plastic bag, to keep oil off your clothes and bedding
  • A hot water bottle or heating pad (optional)
  • An old towel you don't mind staining
Myth

Castor oil packs are a proven cure for arthritis.

What's true

There's very little research on packs for joints. They're best thought of as a warm, restful comfort ritual, not a treatment.

How to make a castor oil pack, step by step

  1. Test your skin firstRub a little castor oil on the inside of your arm and wait a day. If you get a rash or itching, skip it.
  2. Soak the clothPour enough oil onto the flannel to make it damp all the way through, but not dripping.
  3. Lay it over the sore jointKnee, hand, shoulder or hip. Cover it with plastic wrap, then the towel.
  4. Add gentle warmth if you likePut a hot water bottle or heating pad on top, set to warm, not hot. Never fall asleep on a heating pad.
  5. Rest for 30 to 60 minutesPut your feet up, read, or listen to something calm.
  6. Clean up and save the clothWipe your skin with a warm cloth. Keep the flannel in a sealed bag or jar in the fridge and reuse it several times, adding a little oil each time.

Skip the pack if

the skin is broken or irritated, the joint is hot, red and swollen, or you're pregnant. If you take blood thinners or have a skin condition, ask your doctor first.

What the research says

Honest answer: there's very little research on castor oil packs for joints. Most of what we know comes from people's own experience.

The study people quote most often, from 2009, looked at castor oil capsules swallowed by mouth, not packs. About 100 people with knee osteoarthritis took either castor oil capsules or the pain medicine diclofenac for four weeks. Both groups said their knees felt better, and the castor oil group reported fewer side effects.1 A later Cochrane review of herbal treatments noted the study was small and exploratory, and that knee pain improved more in the diclofenac group.2

A castor oil pack isn't a cure. It's half an hour of warmth, rest and quiet care for a sore joint.

Warmth on its own is something many people with stiff joints find helpful, so think of a castor oil pack as a comfort ritual rather than a treatment.

Castor oil for knees, hands, hips and back

A castor oil pack, also called a castor oil compress or castor oil wrap, can be used on sore knees, stiff hands and fingers, an aching hip or a tight lower back. The steps are the same for each: oil-soaked flannel, plastic, a towel and gentle warmth. As a home remedy for sore joints it's cheap and simple.

  • Pick a regular time, like after dinner, so it becomes a habit.

Questions people ask

Does castor oil help arthritis?

There's very little research on castor oil packs for joints. One small 2009 study of castor oil capsules taken by mouth found that knee osteoarthritis symptoms improved, though a later Cochrane review noted that the pain medicine diclofenac did better. Many people find the warmth and rest of a pack soothing, but it isn't a proven treatment.

How long should I leave a castor oil pack on my knee?

Most people rest with it for 30 to 60 minutes. Use gentle warmth, not high heat, and never fall asleep on a heating pad.

Can I leave castor oil on my knees overnight?

Some people rub a little castor oil into the skin at night and wear old socks or leggings. Test your skin first, and don't sleep with a heating pad or hot water bottle on.

Can I use castor oil on my hands and fingers?

Yes. Wrap the fingers or palm in a small oiled cloth, cover with plastic and a warm towel, and rest for half an hour. Skip it on broken or irritated skin.

Is a castor oil compress the same as a castor oil pack?

Yes. Castor oil pack, castor oil compress and castor oil wrap all describe the same thing: an oil-soaked cloth laid over the body and kept warm.

Who should not use a castor oil pack?

Skip it on broken or irritated skin, over a joint that is hot, red and swollen, and during pregnancy. If you take blood thinners or have a skin condition, ask your doctor first.
